Tuition FX Risk Simulator: GBP/LKR

How many extra rupees should a Sri Lankan family set aside today so that a pound-denominated tuition payment is covered with a chosen confidence? This page simulates thousands of possible future exchange-rate paths in your browser and estimates the reserve (Value at Risk) and the average shortfall in bad cases (Expected Shortfall).

About / How to use

How to use

  1. Choose a model, the tuition amount, the months until payment and the confidence level.
  2. Press Start. Paths are simulated in batches of 5,000; the charts and numbers update live. Pause stops the run, and pressing Start again continues it. Reset clears the results (changing any parameter also resets).
  3. Read the outputs: Extra reserve is the number of rupees to set aside today on top of the budget at today's rate, so that the payment is covered with the chosen confidence. Average bad-case shortfall is how much is needed on average when that confidence is exceeded. The ± figure is a 95% confidence interval over batches.

Try this

Keep GBM, 6 months, 95%. Note the reserve. Now switch to the regime model and set the starting regime to Crisis: the reserve rises sharply. That shows why planning during a currency crisis is far more expensive than planning in calm times. Then set the confidence to 99% and compare the two models. The regime model's heavier tail now matters much more.

The model

GBM: dS = μS dt + σS dW, simulated with the exact solution ST = S0 exp((μ − σ²/2)T + σ√T Z), Z ~ N(0,1), for the reserve, and daily exact steps for the drawn paths. The shortfall is L = amount × (ST − S0). VaR is the α-quantile of L; ES is the mean of L beyond the VaR. For GBM the closed-form lognormal VaR is also shown as a check.

Regime switching: a hidden two-state Markov chain (calm/crisis) moves once per CBSL business day (241 per year). Given the regime, the daily log-return is normal with that regime's mean and standard deviation. Fitted by maximum likelihood (Hamilton filter) to CBSL daily rates, 29 Jul 2013 – 20 Nov 2026:

Random numbers come from a seeded xoshiro128** generator, with normal variates from the Box–Muller transform. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

Limitations

  • The CBSL indicative rate excludes the bank's selling spread on telegraphic transfers; add it to the reserve.
  • GBM assumes constant volatility and normal log-returns; real rupee returns have much heavier tails.
  • Neither model can foresee policy-driven breaks such as the March 2022 float of the rupee.
  • Interest earned on the rupee reserve, and hedging with forwards or foreign-currency accounts, are ignored.

Data: Central Bank of Sri Lanka indicative exchange rates, via the Frankfurter API. Educational tool, not financial advice.
